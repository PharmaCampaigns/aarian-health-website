The pharmacy channel is your most underused acquisition engine.

Acquisition5 min read

Most brands treat community pharmacy as a place to be stocked, not a channel to grow through. That framing quietly caps acquisition, because it hands the growth lever to whoever can physically visit the most stores.

Community pharmacy is one of the highest-trust, highest-frequency touchpoints in healthcare. Patients go there routinely, ask for advice, and act on what the pharmacist recommends. For a brand trying to acquire new patients or customers, that is an extraordinary asset. And yet the standard playbook, a field team visiting a priority list of stores, leaves most of that asset untouched.

The maths of the van run

A field representative has a finite number of quality conversations in a day. Multiply that by a team, subtract travel, admin and the stores that were closed or busy, and you arrive at a hard ceiling on reach. Everything beyond that ceiling, often the majority of the network, never hears the message. Acquisition growth becomes a function of headcount and geography, not of the opportunity itself.

When acquisition is capped by drive time, the channel isn't the constraint. The delivery model is.

Unlocking the channel beyond the field team

The channel isn't the constraint; the delivery model is. Unlocking it means separating reach from drive time. That is a combination of three moves:

  • Coordinator-led engagement that reaches the stores a rep would never physically get to, so coverage reflects opportunity rather than logistics.
  • A structured, compliant conversation that any store can run consistently, so the message doesn't degrade as it scales.
  • Technology that frees the field team from repetitive administration, so the reps you do have spend their time selling, not filing.

Done well, this doesn't replace the field team, it multiplies it. The reps focus on the highest-value relationships; the channel does the rest of the work in parallel.

The proof problem, solved

There's a reason finance teams are sceptical of pharmacy programs: historically, they've been hard to measure. Solve that, capture every interaction and surface it as pharmacy claims, reach and ROI in a live dashboard, and the channel stops being an act of faith. It becomes a defensible line in the acquisition plan, with the evidence to earn more investment next cycle.

If your acquisition is bumping against the limits of your field team, the answer may not be more reps. It may be a delivery model that finally lets the pharmacy channel do what it's capable of.
