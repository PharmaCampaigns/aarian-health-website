The nuance

One decision. A dozen influences.

A consumer health purchase at the counter is the end of a long chain: a GP or allied health professional who mentioned it, an assistant who was asked first, a pharmacist whose word carries weight, packaging that had to earn a second look, and a category buyer who decided it would be on the shelf at all.

The counter is not the start

By the time someone reaches the pharmacy, the decision has usually been shaped somewhere else: a consultation, a search, a conversation at home.

The assistant is asked first

The pharmacy assistant is usually the first person a customer speaks to, and the one who recognises when a question belongs with the pharmacist. A campaign has to work for both, and they need different things from it.

Ranging comes before all of it

None of it matters until the category buyer and the banner have agreed you belong on the shelf.

How engagement works

In chronic metabolic health, engagement is a long game.

These are lifelong conditions, so the work is the same shape every time: acquire the patient or user, activate them into real use and behaviour change, then retain them for the long haul. We define those points and work across all three.

Acquisition

Looking to win new patients or users?

The thing that keeps brand managers awake at night. Whether it is a launch that needs traction, a product that needs speed, or a category that has gone quiet, we design the acquisition play and deliver it, finding the patients and users who should be on your product and bringing them in.

Activation

Got attention but not action?

You can lead an elephant to water, but you cannot make it drink. When a product is in hand yet usage lags and behaviour does not change, we weave human conversation and technology together to unlock the X factor and turn intent into real, sustained use, while keeping medical and compliance comfortable.

Retention

How do you keep them coming back?

The holy grail. For lifelong conditions, retention is everything. We design and run the data and lifecycle work that keeps patients and users engaged over the long haul, the hardest to earn, the most rewarding when it is done right, and the one that makes for some great slides at the annual review.
