Compliance isn't the enemy of engagement.
It's the enabler.

Compliance5 min read

In too many organisations, compliance is where good campaigns go to slow down. It arrives at the end, as a review, and the team braces for the list of changes. That sequence, not the requirement itself, is the real problem.

Medical, regulatory and compliance colleagues aren't there to say no. They're there to make sure a program is safe, ethical and defensible, for patients, for the brand and for the professionals delivering it. Those are the same conditions a program needs to succeed. The friction people complain about doesn't come from the standard; it comes from meeting the standard too late.

The cost of reviewing it in at the end

When compliance is the final gate, three things happen. Work gets redone, because issues surface after the creative and the mechanics are already built. Timelines slip, because the fix touches decisions made months earlier. And trust erodes, because commercial and medical teams end up on opposite sides of a deadline instead of the same side of a goal.

The friction isn't the standard. It's meeting the standard too late.

Building it in from the first conversation

The alternative is to treat compliance as a design input, present from the first whiteboard rather than the final sign-off. In practice that means a few disciplines become part of how a program is built, not checks applied to it afterwards:

  • Version and document control so everyone is always working from the approved asset, and the audit trail writes itself.
  • Adverse event pathways designed into the workflow, so pharmacovigilance obligations are met automatically, not remembered.
  • Business continuity and governance considered up front, so the program is robust before it scales, not patched after an incident.

None of this slows a good program down. It's what allows it to move quickly, because the hard questions have already been answered, and to scale, because every store, message and interaction is delivered inside the same guardrails.

Passing the headline test

The simplest way to think about it: every program should be designed to pass the headline test, to look entirely defensible if it were described on the front page. Build to that standard from the start and compliance stops being a brake. It becomes the thing that lets commercial teams be ambitious with confidence.

Engagement and compliance were never really in tension. Sequenced correctly, each makes the other stronger.
